How to Start, Stop & Transfer Atmos Gas Service

Whether you're moving into a new home, transferring service to another address, or disconnecting gas before you leave, this guide walks you through exactly what to expect. Real timelines, actual problems, and how to get help if things get stuck.

Start Here: The Quick Version

Starting Atmos gas service means entering your address, picking your move-in date, proving you live there or own the place, and making sure your meter is ready. From request to gas flowing is usually 1 to 3 business days. But if they need to inspect the line or install a meter, add more time.

Not working on move-in day? It's usually because the meter isn't installed yet, inspection is pending, or your address isn't recognized in their system. Call (856) 900-7834 to find out what's holding it up.

Transferring service? Give them your current account, new address, and transfer date. They'll check if the new place has a meter or needs one.

Stopping service? Request your disconnect date a few days before you move out. They'll schedule a final meter read and close your account.

Starting Service

How to Start Atmos Gas Service

Step 1: Have Your Address Ready

Write down your full street address exactly as it appears on mail or official documents. Include the apartment number or unit if you have one. Atmos needs the address to match their database, or they'll ask you to double-check before moving forward.

Step 2: Pick Your Start or Move-In Date

Choose the date you want gas turned on. The earlier you request service before that date, the better your chances of being ready. If you're moving in tomorrow, call them today or they might not make it.

Step 3: Verify You Live There or Own It

Atmos will ask for ID and proof you're authorized to request service. Renters might need a lease. Homeowners might need a closing document or deed. Have ID ready when you call or apply online.

Step 4: Confirm Your Meter and Service Line

They'll check if a gas meter already exists at your address. If yes, they just turn it on. If no, they'll schedule someone to install one. That's usually where delays happen—meter installs take longer than just activating an existing meter.

Step 5: Wait for Inspection (If Needed)

If your place has never had gas service, or if the line hasn't been checked in a long time, Atmos may send someone to inspect the meter connection and gas lines for safety. This is required by law. Most inspections happen the same day or next day, but if they're busy, it might be a few days.

Step 6: Gas Gets Turned On

Once inspection passes (or if inspection isn't needed), they activate service. You should have heat, hot water, and your stove working within the timeline they gave you.

How Long Does It Actually Take?

Situation Typical Time
Meter already exists, no inspection needed 1-2 business days
Meter exists, inspection required 2-4 business days
New meter installation 3-7 business days
New meter + inspection 5-10 business days

These timelines assume you submitted everything correctly and Atmos is on their normal schedule. If you're requesting service late in the week, or if the address is causing issues, add a few more days.

Same-Day Service

Can You Get Same-Day Atmos Gas Service?

Straight answer: rarely. Same-day gas activation happens only if:

  • A meter already exists at your address
  • Inspection is not required (or already passed)
  • Your request comes in early enough for them to activate it before they close for the day
  • The service area isn't slammed with other requests

If you're in a rush, call as early as possible and ask directly: "Can you activate today?" If the answer is no, ask what's blocking it. Usually it's the meter or the inspection.

Real move-in panic? If gas is off and you have no heat or hot water, mention that when you call. They might prioritize your inspection.
Move-In Problems

Why Gas Isn't Active on Move-In Day

You're moving in Saturday and gas still isn't on. Here's what went wrong:

1. Address Isn't in Their System Yet

Sometimes new addresses, recently constructed homes, or properties that changed owners take time to show up in Atmos's database. The fix: Call them and read your full address from your deed, closing documents, or mail. Ask them to manually look it up.

2. No Meter or Meter Not Ready

If someone lived there before and the meter was disconnected, or if the property never had gas, they need to install one. You can't turn on gas without a meter. This is the most common hold-up. Ask when the meter install is scheduled.

3. Inspection is Pending

A service tech needs to check the gas line and meter connection before activation. If inspection is scheduled for Monday but you move in Saturday, you're stuck. Call and ask if they can do it Friday afternoon or if they have an emergency inspection slot.

4. Your Verification Didn't Go Through

You sent ID, but they didn't receive it, or it didn't match their records. If the name on your ID doesn't match the account, lease, or deed, they'll pause and ask for clarification. Have your lease or homeowner's paperwork ready.

5. Old Account or Prior Disconnect Still Active

If someone else lived there and had Atmos service, their old account might still be flagged. You need a new account. This is annoying but fixable—call and explain the property changed hands or tenants.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Call (856) 900-7834 and ask: "What is preventing activation?"
  2. Get a specific answer: "Meter not installed," "Inspection pending," "Address not recognized," etc.
  3. Ask what day/time they can fix it
  4. Get a name or reference number in case you need to follow up
Inspections

Do You Need a Gas Line Inspection?

Atmos requires a safety inspection in most cases. Here's why and when:

  • New meter installs: Almost always inspected before activation
  • First-time gas service at a property: Required
  • Lines haven't been used in years: They'll want to check for leaks or damage
  • Existing meter but new account: Sometimes they inspect, sometimes not

What the inspector does: They check the meter connection, gas lines inside and outside the house, and look for leaks or safety issues. It takes about 30 minutes to an hour. You should be home but don't need to do anything—let them work.

Cost: Inspection is free as part of starting service. You're not charged separately.

Schedule in advance if possible. Inspection slots fill up, especially on weekends. Tuesday or Wednesday afternoons are usually easier to get.
Stop Service

How to Stop or Disconnect Atmos Gas Service

Whether you're moving out or just don't want gas service anymore, here's how to shut it down:

Step 1: Call and Request a Disconnect Date

Don't wait until your moving day. Call at least 3-5 days before you want to stop service. Give them a specific date. They need time to schedule a technician to turn off the meter.

Step 2: Confirm the Final Meter Read

Ask them to confirm they'll do a final meter read on the disconnect date. This closes out your account and finalizes your last bill. Without it, the account might stay open and they could charge you for time after you move.

Step 3: Settle Your Account

Make sure there are no past-due charges on your account before you leave. If there's a balance, pay it. Ask about your security deposit if you put one down when you started service—it might be refunded after the final bill posts.

Step 4: Get Confirmation

Before you move out, get written or recorded confirmation that service was disconnected. Keep that documentation. If a bill shows up after you move, you have proof.

Common mistake: Requesting disconnect on the same day you're moving. If they can't get someone out that day, the account stays active and you'll be charged. Plan ahead.
Transfer Service

How to Transfer Atmos Gas Service to a New Address

If you're staying with Atmos but moving to a different address in their service area, a transfer is often simpler than starting a brand new account.

What You'll Need

  • Your current Atmos account number
  • Your new service address
  • Your move-in or transfer date
  • Any updated ID if your name has changed

The Process

  1. Call and tell them you're transferring, not starting new
  2. Give them the new address and confirm it's in Atmos service territory
  3. Set the transfer date
  4. They'll check if the new place has a meter and if inspection is needed
  5. Confirm the old address will be disconnected on the same date or a few days after

One Thing to Watch

The new address might not have a meter, just like starting new service. If they need to install one, the timeline extends. Ask up front: "Does the new address already have a gas meter?" If not, plan for extra days.

Pro tip: Request transfer at least a week before you move. That gives them time to check the new place and plan the installation or inspection if needed.
Common Problems

Other Reasons Your Gas Isn't Working

Meter Exists but Gas Won't Turn On

The meter is there but no gas is flowing. Possible reasons:

  • Meter valve is shut, not activated
  • There's an active "do not turn on" flag from a previous issue
  • The line has a leak and failed inspection
  • Old account hasn't been fully closed

Fix: Call and ask directly: "Why can't the meter be turned on?" Get the specific reason.

Gas Turns On Then Off

Service was on but then turned back off. Usually because:

  • Inspection failed and they found a leak or safety issue
  • Payment didn't go through and the account was flagged
  • There's a gas leak in your home (they'll shut it off for safety)
  • Wrong account was activated by mistake

Fix: Call immediately and ask what happened. If it's a leak, don't turn the gas back on yourself.

FAQ

Questions People Actually Ask

How much does it cost to start Atmos gas service?

There's usually no charge to activate service. If they need to install a new meter or service line, there may be an installation or connection fee. Ask when you call.

Do I need a security deposit?

Depends on your credit and their policy. They might hold a deposit equal to 1-2 months of estimated usage. It gets refunded after a year if you pay on time.

Can I start service online?

Some addresses work online. Others require a phone call. Try online first. If it doesn't work, call (856) 900-7834.

What if the house is a rental?

As a renter, you can usually request service in your name if the lease allows it. You might need to show your lease. Some landlords keep gas in their name—ask before you call.

What if I just need to verify my service is active?

Call (856) 900-7834 with your account number or address. They can check your status in real time.

What do I do if there's a gas leak?

Stop using gas immediately. Don't light any flames. Leave the building if the smell is strong. Call 911 or Atmos emergency line. This is not normal—get professional help.

Can I move my meter to a different location on my property?

Usually no. The meter must be in a specific location for safety and accessibility. If you want it moved, it requires a service tech and will cost money. Not recommended unless absolutely necessary.

What happens if I don't show up for my inspection appointment?

Service activation will be delayed. They'll reschedule, but you'll lose time. Be there or call ahead if you can't make it.
